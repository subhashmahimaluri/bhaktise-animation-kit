Almost every town in India has a temple for Shiva. Almost every town has a temple for Vishnu.

But Brahma created the whole world. So where are his temples?

There are only a handful. The best known is in Pushkar, in Rajasthan.

Why? The Shiva Purana gives the answer. And the answer begins with a swan.

High above the world, a white swan was flying upward. That swan was Brahma himself.

He had been flying for a very long time. Beside him rose a pillar of fire. Up, and up, and up. His wings were tired. But he still could not see the top.

Then he saw something falling past him. A flower. A sweet-smelling ketaki flower, drifting slowly down.

And Brahma had an idea.

What happens when the creator himself tells one small lie?

To understand, we must go back to the beginning. It began with a quarrel.

Lord Vishnu was resting on the great serpent Shesha, in deep sleep. Brahma came by and saw him.

"Get up," said Brahma. "See who has come. I am your elder. You should rise and greet me."

Vishnu opened his eyes. He was not pleased, but he spoke politely. "Come, sit down." Then he added, "But remember one thing. The whole universe rests inside me. Even you were born from the lotus that grew from my navel."

"I am the lord," said Brahma.

"No. I am the lord," said Vishnu.

That is all it was. Two great gods, and one small question. Who is greater?

Words became weapons. Vishnu rose on his eagle, Garuda. Brahma rose on his swan. Arrows flew across the sky. Then each of them reached for his most terrible weapon. Fire spread everywhere. The three worlds began to burn.

The gods were frightened. They ran to Mount Kailasa and fell at the feet of Shiva.

"Lord, save us. If these two do not stop, nothing will be left."

Shiva comforted them, the way a father comforts frightened children. Then he set out for the battlefield.

Brahma and Vishnu raised their weapons one more time.

And suddenly, between the two of them, a pillar of fire rose up.

It was huge. It blazed with flames. It went down through the earth, below all the worlds beneath. It went up through the sky, beyond all the worlds above.

Both their weapons flew into it... and vanished.

The fighting stopped. Both of them stared.

"What is this?" they said. "Where does it begin? Where does it end? Let us find out."

So they agreed. One would go down. One would go up.

Vishnu took the form of a great boar. He dug into the earth and went down. Past one world below. Past another. Deeper, and deeper.

Brahma took the form of a swan and flew up. Higher, and higher.

Years passed. Many, many years.

Vishnu kept digging. But he could not find the bottom of the pillar. At last, tired out, he turned back.

And up in the sky, Brahma was just as tired. That is when he saw the ketaki flower.

"O flower," he said, "where have you come from?"

"I have been falling from this pillar for many, many years," said the flower. "And I have still not reached the ground."

Brahma understood. He would never reach the top.

But he did not want to lose.

He thought, "If I go back and say I failed, Vishnu will be the greater one."

So he said to the flower, "Do one thing for me. Come down with me. Tell Vishnu that I saw the top of this pillar. Be my witness."

The ketaki flower agreed.

Down below, the two gods met again.

Vishnu spoke first. And he spoke the truth.

"I went as far as I could. I did not find the bottom."

Brahma lifted his head proudly.

"I found the top. I saw it with my own eyes. If you do not believe me, ask this flower."

"Yes," said the ketaki flower. "He saw it."

Vishnu believed them. He bowed to Brahma and began to worship him.

At that very moment... the pillar of fire opened.

And out of the fire stepped Lord Shiva himself.

The pillar had been Shiva all along.

Vishnu trembled. He held Shiva's feet.

"Lord, we fought out of ignorance. Each of us wanted to be the greatest. Forgive us."

Shiva looked at him kindly.

"Vishnu, you wanted to be the lord. And still, you did not give up the truth. So people will honour you as they honour me. You will have your own temples, your own images, your own festivals."

Then Shiva turned towards Brahma.

His face changed. From the space between his eyebrows, a fierce form appeared. Dark and terrible, with a sword in his hand. His name was Bhairava.

Bhairava caught Brahma by the hair and raised his sword.

In those days, the Shiva Purana says, Brahma had five heads. The fifth head was the one that had told the lie. Bhairava removed it. That is why we see Brahma with four heads.

Brahma shook with fear and fell at Bhairava's feet.

And then someone stepped forward to save him.

It was Vishnu. The very one Brahma had lied to.

"Lord," Vishnu said to Shiva, "forgive him. This is his first mistake."

Shiva stopped Bhairava. Brahma was safe. But Shiva spoke firmly.

"You wanted worship, and you used a lie to get it. So you will have no temples of your own, and no festivals of your own."

Brahma bowed his head. His pride was gone.

"Lord, be kind to me," he said.

And Shiva was kind.

"You will be the teacher of every yajna, every sacred fire ceremony. Without you, no yajna will be complete."

And the ketaki flower? It had given false witness. So Shiva said, "You will have no place in my worship."

The flower begged for mercy. Shiva softened a little.

"My attendants may wear you. You may decorate the canopy above me. But you will not be offered to me in worship."

That is why, even today, the ketaki flower is not offered in the worship of Shiva.

So there were three of them, and three results.

Vishnu told the truth. He received temples.

Brahma told a lie. He lost his.

The flower repeated the lie. It lost its place in worship.

Then Shiva spoke about the pillar of fire.

"This pillar has no beginning and no end. But I will make it small, so that the world can see it and worship it. This form of fire will become a hill. Its name will be Arunachala, the red hill. Those who live there, and even those who remember me there, will find freedom."

That hill still stands. It is in Tamil Nadu, in the town of Tiruvannamalai.

Every year, on the full moon of the Tamil month of Karthigai, the temple there remembers the pillar of fire. In the evening, a great lamp of ghee is lit on the very top of the hill. It can be seen for miles around.

Pilgrims walk around the whole hill. And all over Tamil Nadu, families light rows of clay lamps at their doors.

This festival is called Karthigai Deepam.

Now think about the story once more.

Brahma flew higher than anyone. Vishnu dug deeper than anyone. Neither of them found the end.

But the one who said, "I did not find it," was the one who was honoured.

One small lie cost the creator his temples. One simple truth gave Vishnu his.

The next time you light a lamp, remember this. That light has no top and no bottom. And no lie can ever reach it.

Om Namah Shivaya.
